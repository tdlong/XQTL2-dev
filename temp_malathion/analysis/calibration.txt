Replicate 1 only, the same four pools through each test.

PART 1  Where each test gets its yardstick, at the published peaks
  chr2R  signal MS 0.0176  error MS 0.00169  F 10.4
  chr3L  signal MS 0.0109  error MS 0.00115  F  9.5
  2R carries 1.6x the signal of 3L but also 1.5x the residual, so the two F
  values nearly match. The ANOVA's residual is measured at that window and
  grows when the replicates disagree. The Wald's variance is modelled from
  pool size, is near enough equal at both peaks, and so lets the 1.6x in
  signal pass straight through. That is the whole of 5.1 -> 6.9.

PART 2  Genome-wide calibration, QTL regions removed

         test n_windows median_mlog10p lambda frac_above_2 frac_above_3
   2022 ANOVA      4480          0.508  2.260       0.0404      0.00446
 current Wald     21900          0.159  0.342       0.0000      0.00000

  lambda is the median chi-square over its null expectation: 1 is calibrated.
  Under a correct null the median -log10P is 0.30 and 10% of windows exceed 1.

PART 3  Do the replicates agree as closely as pool size predicts?

  predicted variance of the female-minus-male shift, from 43 and 65 flies: 0.00966
  observed median squared difference, genome-wide, QTL removed:          0.00290
  ratio observed / predicted: 0.30

  Above 1 means the pools differ by more than counting flies explains, so a
  variance built only from pool size understates the real one. Part of any
  excess is haplotype reconstruction error, which the Wald does model, so
  this ratio is an upper bound on what the Wald misses.


PART 4  chr2R alone, both pipelines, replicate 1, data held fixed

  signal-to-noise at the peak window is F = 10.40 either way.

  as F(7,16), denominator ESTIMATED from the replicates : -log10P =  4.20
  as chi-square, same ratio, denominator treated as KNOWN: -log10P = 12.40
     => estimating the denominator on 16 df costs 8.2 units

  the Wald reports -log10P = 6.92, i.e. chi-square 45.3 on 7 df,
  against 72.8 if its variance equalled the ANOVA residual,
     => the Wald's modelled variance is about 1.61x larger, worth -5.5 units

  So the gap is the small residue of two large opposing effects: +8 for not
  paying a degrees-of-freedom penalty, -5 for a bigger variance. With only
  two replicate pairs an empirical denominator is both expensive and poorly
  estimated, which is the fundamental reason the modelled variance wins here.


PART 5  Is the Wald's denominator too small or too big?

  21887 null windows, statistic is chi-square on 7 df
    median observed 4.725   expected 6.346
    mean   observed 5.279   expected 7.000
    => the denominator is about 1.34x too LARGE, not too small

  chr2R peak as reported: -log10P 6.92, chi-square 45.3
  with the denominator rescaled by that factor: -log10P 10.0

  So 6.9 is a floor. Correcting the denominator to the size the null says
  it should be raises the peak, it does not lower it. The 2022 denominator
  errs the other way: too small, by the sum-to-one effect in PART 4's
  simulation and by the locally low estimate at that window.

  Measured on replicate 1 only, where pools are 43 and 65 flies. The
  denominator has a multinomial part that shrinks as 1/N and a smoothing
  and reconstruction part that does not, so this factor is not portable to
  replicate 2's much larger pools. That is the measurement that matters for
  the 19.6, and the 8-pool run supplies it.
