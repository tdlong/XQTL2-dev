Malathion X-QTL · reanalysis note

Why Cyp6g1 Grew Fourfold

The 2022 data, run through the current pipeline, give a peak near 20 where the paper reported just above 5. The reads, the SNP calls and the haplotype frequencies are not what changed. The test is.

Long lab · XQTL2 25 November 2026 Macdonald & Long 2022, G3 12:jkac279

Summary

All eight pools of the 2022 experiment were re-fetched from the SRA and run end to end through the current pipeline. It reproduces the larger peak: 19.6 at Cyp6g1 and 5.2 at Mdr65. The published test, applied to those same new haplotype frequencies, reproduces the published figure: 5.1 and 4.7.

Same reads, same SNP calls, same haplotype frequencies, two tests, both published pictures. Nothing in the data or the haplotype caller contributes to the discrepancy.

The two tests disagree about one thing: what a larger pool is worth. The 2022 test cannot use the extra flies in replicate 2. The current test can, and three independent checks say its yardstick is close to correct while the 2022 one is too small.

What was run

The eight pools of BioProject PRJNA857080 were downloaded from the ENA mirror, checked against the archive's MD5 sums, aligned with bwa mem, counted against a catalogue of SNPs segregating among the eight pA founder strains, and resolved into founder haplotype frequencies in 50 kb windows smoothed over 250 kb. Nothing from the original analysis was reused.

For the comparison, the paper's own ANOVA_test_function was taken verbatim from its figshare deposit, including the rule that drops founders below about 2% at each window. Applied to the paper's own deposited frequencies it returns 5.11 at 65.8 cM and 4.91 at 16.1 cM, against published peaks of just above 5 and about 4.9. The published test is therefore implemented correctly here, and every comparison below is like for like.

The haplotype frequencies did not change

Taking the four replicate-1 pools, which exist under both pipelines, the founder frequency shifts at the two peaks agree founder by founder and sex by sex at r = 0.996 on 2R and 0.995 on 3L. The sum of squared shifts matches to within a few percent. Whatever changed, the estimate of what the flies did did not.

What the extra flies are worth

The published test asks whether the four control-selected pairs agree with one another, measured against how much such pairs disagree. Its yardstick is the scatter among the pairs, and that scatter does not shrink when a pool contains 700 flies instead of 43. The test therefore cannot distinguish a frequency shift measured on 43 flies from the same shift measured on 700.

The current test asks whether the shift exceeds the sampling noise expected from the number of flies actually pooled. A pool of 700 has far less sampling error than a pool of 43, so the same shift is far better established. That is ordinary binomial sampling.

Replicate 2 held 264 control flies and 693 or 403 selected, against 43 and 65 in replicate 1. Most of the experiment's information sits there, and the published analysis could not reach it. Two observations make this concrete rather than rhetorical.

Adding replicate 2 does not move the published test. At Cyp6g1 it gives 5.11 on replicate 1 alone and 5.09 on all eight pools. A second, much larger experiment changed the answer by 0.02.

Run each pair alone and the pattern inverts. The replicate-2 pairs carry visibly smaller frequency shifts than the replicate-1 pairs, and yield roughly twice the evidence, because they counted roughly six times as many flies.

Which yardstick is right

This is the part that matters, and it is measurable rather than arguable. Across the roughly 95% of the genome carrying no QTL, a correctly calibrated test produces a known distribution of values. The current test's statistic is a chi-square on seven degrees of freedom, so its median across null windows should be 6.346.

The published test's yardstick fails in the opposite direction, for two reasons that are structural rather than incidental.

  • It treats the eight founder frequencies at a locus as eight independent numbers. They are proportions constrained to sum to one, and so are negatively correlated. Simulating a null with no QTL and no reconstruction error at all, the test still rejects at 8.4% when it should reject at 5%. Running the identical model on eight genuinely independent values per pool restores calibration exactly, which identifies the constraint as the cause.
  • It cannot see uncertainty in the haplotype estimate itself. Its denominator is the variation between pools, so reconstruction error shared by all pools at a locus never enters it, while it does move the numerator. On the published frequencies the test's inflation is measurably worse at windows where the haplotype fit sits on a constraint boundary and founders are least separable.

There is a third contribution, smaller but in the same direction. The 2022 denominator is re-estimated at every window from sixteen residual degrees of freedom and is not smoothed. Neighbouring windows overlap by 97%, so that estimate barely moves between them, but at 1.5 cM, where windows stop sharing data, it wanders by exactly the amount pure estimation noise predicts. It is essentially all noise, correlated over one window width, which is also the width of a QTL peak. At Cyp6g1 that noise happened to fall low, worth about one unit of the published 5.11.

The published 5.1 is therefore not a conservative version of 19.6. It is a different and worse measurement that happens to be smaller.

What this changes, and what it does not

It does not change the biology. The founder shifts are the same to within half a percent: A5 and A6 rise under selection and A7 falls at Cyp6g1, consistently in all four pairs, with A5 at +0.17, +0.25, +0.04 and +0.09 and A7 at −0.02, −0.13, −0.12 and −0.14. The QTL intervals, the candidate genes and the conclusions of the paper all stand. A larger −log10P is more confidence in the same effect, not a bigger effect.

What it does change is that the 2022 experiment was better powered than the published analysis was able to show, because the analysis could not spend replicate 2's flies. It also means the paper's genome-wide threshold of 4 does not transfer: it was calibrated by simulation for a test whose null distribution is different, and the two numbers are not on a common scale.

One limitation is worth stating plainly. Both tests assume the pools differ only by selection, and neither can detect a batch effect that mimics it. Here that would have to push the same founders in the same direction in two experiments run 48 generations apart, prepared separately and sequenced on different read lengths, and land on the most replicated insecticide resistance locus in the organism. It is not a serious alternative, but it is the assumption both analyses rest on.

Reproducing this

Everything above is in the XQTL2-dev repository. The scan comparison and the paper's test re-implemented from its deposit are in temp_malathion/analysis/compare_scans.R; the agreement between haplotype callers in compare_shifts.R; the calibration measurements in calibration.R; the null simulation and its independence control in null_sim.R; the denominator noise analysis in denominator_noise.R. The eight-pool run itself is scripts_oneoffs/malathion/go_2022.sh, and its outputs are under logs/malathion/.

Published haplotype frequencies and analysis code from Macdonald & Long 2022 were taken from the figshare supplement, doi:10.25387/g3.21200395. Raw reads are NCBI BioProject PRJNA857080.
