Wald scores establish that a region shows significant changes in founder haplotype frequencies, but do not estimate the magnitude of each region's contribution, and given the sample size small haplotype frequency shifts can be highly significant. Because the population derives from eight inbred founders and the selected proportion for each cage is known, the squared changes in founder frequency per window can be used to estimate the heritability explained by that window (Methods), which places the scan on a scale of phenotypic variance (Fig. 1b). Although heritability estimates are necessarily positive and 1cM tiles whose Wald score does not exceed 2 are associated with h^2 estimates of 0.60–0.66% depending on the trait, h^2 estimates of 0.90–1.04% are associated with significant -log10 P values of 7.5, while estimates of 1.13–1.51% are associated with highly significant -log10 P values of 15. The highly significant chr3L locus reaches 4.5% of phenotypic variance in high-sugar males and 2.2% in low-sugar females, but the next largest regions although highly significant only reach h^2 values of 1.5–2.3%. Taking the strongest trait at each tile, the median tile carries 1.0% of phenotypic variance and three quarters carry less than 1.3%. Resolving contributions of this magnitude required screening ~104,000 flies per treatment, selecting the longest-lived 5.2% and sequencing 22,896 selected individuals. Although these regions represent substantial fractions of heritability for longevity, even if each local peak were explained by a single causative locus it would likely have gone undetected in the modestly sized mapping panels of several hundred RILs (cites), or association type panels consisting of 100s of ILs or 1000s of genotyped individuals. It is striking that variation throughout the genome in the -log10 P Wald scores are congruent with attempts to reconcile GWAS and affected sib linkage mapping results in humans (Sidenko).
