Longevity is highly polygenic. Wald scores establish that a region shows significant changes in founder haplotype frequencies, but do not estimate the magnitude of each region's contribution, and given the sample size small shifts can be highly significant. Because the population derives from eight inbred founders and the selected proportion for each cage is known, the squared changes in founder frequency per window provides an estimate of the heritability explained by that window (Methods), placing the scan on a scale of phenotypic variance (Fig. 1b). Heritability estimates are sums of squares and so are positive by construction: a window at which nothing has happened still returns a positive value, and across windows whose Wald score never exceeds 2 that floor sits at 0.60–0.66% depending on the trait. Comparing the two scales per trait shows where estimates rise clear of it. Estimates near 1% are associated with Wald scores of 7.5 to 15 and so reflect shifts that are clearly significant, whereas estimates around 0.8% correspond to a Wald score of 5 and are suggestive at best. Individual contributions are small on this scale. The chr3L locus reaches 4.5% of phenotypic variance in high-sugar males and 2.2% in low-sugar females, and the next largest regions reach 1.5–2.3%. Taking the strongest trait at each tile, the median tile carries 1.0% of phenotypic variance and three quarters carry less than 1.3%. Resolving contributions of this magnitude required screening ~104,000 flies per treatment, selecting the longest-lived 5.2% and sequencing 22,896 selected individuals. Although these regions represent substantial fractions of heritability for longevity, even if each local peak were explained by a single causative locus it would likely have gone undetected in the modestly sized mapping panels of several hundred RILs (cites), or association type panels consisting of 100s of ILs or 1000s of genotyped individuals. It is striking that variation throughout the genome in the -log10 P Wald scores are congruent with attempts to reconcile GWAS and affected sib linkage mapping results in humans (Sidenko).
