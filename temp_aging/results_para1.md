Much of the genome contributes to haplotype change in long lived individuals. Each sex × diet group was scanned independently, contrasting the longest-lived 3–11% of each of 10 replicates per treatment with an unselected sample from the same cage ("same cage" not exactly how it was done, but can be described in detail in M&M) (Fig. 1a). Dividing the genetic map into 268 not wholly heterochromatic 1 cM tiles at a -log10(p) Wald threshold of 5, 56% of tiles respond in at least one group, 23% exceed a threshold of 10 and 12% exceed 15. The region on chr3L @9.3 Mb is exceptional, it is highly significant in all four treatments and reaches a −log10 P of 163.8 in high-sugar males. Because the mapping population is an advanced- rather than a single-generation cross, founder haplotypes are broken into short blocks and the stronger peaks are correspondingly narrow. We defined eight and 15 per trait peaks with local maximum -log10 P values exceeding 15 or 7.5 respectively (Table S1). Constructing 2 -log10 P value support intervals for each of the 23 peaks results in 80% of peaks falling between 0.26 and 1.54 cM, and between 167 kb and 919 kb.
