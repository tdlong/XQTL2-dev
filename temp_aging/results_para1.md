Much of the genome contributes to haplotype change in long lived individuals. Each sex × diet group was scanned independently, contrasting the longest-lived 3–11% of each of 10 replicates per treatment with an unselected sample from the same cage ("same cage" not exactly how it was done, but can be described in detail in M&M) (Fig. 1a). Dividing the genetic map into 1 cM tiles and keeping the 268 that are not wholly heterochromatic, at a -log10(p) Wald threshold of 5, 56% of tiles respond in at least one group, 23% exceed a threshold of 10 and 12% exceed 15. The region on chr3L @9.3 Mb is exceptional and reaches a −log10 P of 163.8 in high-sugar males (and is highly significant across all 4 treatments). Because the mapping population is an advanced-generation rather than a single-generation cross, founder haplotypes are broken into short blocks and the stronger peaks are correspondingly narrow. Peaks were defined top-down on the maximum across the four traits, each at least 5 cM from any stronger peak, and their support intervals read off the scan as the span within two −log10 P of the peak (Table S1). Eight peaks exceed −log10 P = 15, with a median interval of 0.39 cM and 270 kb; a further fifteen fall between 7.5 and 15, with a median of 0.73 cM and 245 kb.
