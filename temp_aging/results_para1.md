Much of the genome contributes to haplotype change in long lived individuals. Each sex × diet group was scanned independently, contrasting the longest-lived 3–11% of each of 10 replicates per treatment with an unselected sample from the same cage ("same cage" not exactly how it was done, but can be described in detail in M&M) (Fig. 1a). Dividing the genetic map into 1 cM tiles and keeping the 268 that are not wholly heterochromatic, at a -log10(p) Wald threshold of 5, 56% of tiles respond in at least one group, 23% exceed a threshold of 10 and 12% exceed 15. The region on chr3L @9.3 Mb is exceptional and reaches a −log10 P of 163.8 in high-sugar males (and is highly significant across all 4 treatments). Because the mapping population is an advanced-generation rather than a single-generation cross, founder haplotypes are broken into short blocks and the stronger peaks are correspondingly narrow. Eight well-separated peaks exceed −log10 P = 15. Their two-unit support intervals are consistent in genetic distance — a median of 0.39 cM, and 0.16–0.83 cM for seven of the eight — but span 55 kb to 1.5 Mb of sequence, because physical width tracks local recombination rate.
