Much of the genome contributes to haplotype change in long lived individuals. Each sex × diet group was scanned independently, contrasting the longest-lived 3–11% of each of 10 replicates per treatment with an unselected sample from the same cage ("same cage" not exactly how it was done, but can be described in detail in M&M) (Fig. 1a). Dividing the genetic map into 268 not wholly heterochromatic 1 cM tiles at a -log10(p) Wald threshold of 7.5, 37% of tiles respond in at least one group and 12% exceed a threshold of 15. Because the mapping population is an advanced- rather than a single-generation cross, founder haplotypes are broken into short blocks and the stronger peaks are correspondingly narrow. We defined eight and 15 peaks with local maximum -log10 P values exceeding 15 or 7.5 respectively greater than 5cM apart from one another (Table S1). Constructing 2 -log10 P value support intervals for each of the 23 peaks results in an 80% confidence interval on width of 0.26 to 1.54 cM (167 kb to 919 kb).
