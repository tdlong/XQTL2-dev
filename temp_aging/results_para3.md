Architecture differs between the sexes but is largely invariant to diet. Of the 268 one cM tiles, 98 reach a -log10 P of 7.5 in at least one group, and each was characterised by the single window at which it peaks so that all four groups are compared at the same position. Male heritability exceeds female at every one of the 15 significant tiles on the X, with medians of 0.82% against -0.02%; the X chromosome is hemizygous in Drosophila males, exposing recessive variation that a second chromosome would mask. On the autosomes neither sex is systematically higher, males exceeding females at 52% of 83 tiles with medians of 0.32% against 0.34%, but this is not because the sexes agree: which tiles contribute differs between them. Splitting the ten replicates of each treatment into the five odd and the five even gives two independent estimates of every tile's heritability, and hence a pure error term, against which the heritability at each tile can be partitioned into a component shared by all four groups and components differing by sex or by diet (Fig. 1c). The upward bias in a heritability estimate is a property of the pool it came from, and the four groups differ in how many flies were selected and in how well their founder frequencies reconstruct, so each pool's own estimated bias is subtracted before any contrast is taken; leaving it in halves every fraction below. Over the significant tiles 15.3% (95% CI 10.2-24.8) of the variation among the four groups is sex-specific and 1.6% (0.7-3.7) diet-specific, with no detectable interaction (-0.4%, -0.8 to 0.2); over the autosomes alone the sex-specific fraction is 12.8% (8.0-19.1). This fraction is invisible to any analysis that pools sexes and treats environmental variables as covariates, as would be the case in human genetics where the environment cannot typically be manipulated and can vary geographically with genotype. Although longevity in Drosophila under two sugar diets is only a single example, if this observation is general it would seem to have implications for polygenic risk scores.
