One region stands apart from the polygenic background. The peak on chromosome 3L at 9.30 Mb reaches a -log10 P of 163.8 in high-sugar males and exceeds 36 in all four groups, in a genome where only 12% of tiles exceed 15 in any group, and it accounts for 4.5% of phenotypic variance in high-sugar males and 2.2% in low-sugar females, two to three times the largest of the other regions we detect. It is also the most tightly localised of the peaks in Table S1, with a two -log10 P support interval of 55 kb, or 0.22 cM, between 9.28 and 9.34 Mb. The founder haplotype frequencies at that position show why (Fig. 2d). Founder B3 is at 15.1% in the unselected controls and is the only founder whose frequency falls consistently in the long-lived, by 6.3 percentage points in low-sugar females to 10.3 in high-sugar males; that is a 41 to 69% reduction in its representation among the longest-lived flies, and two to three times the largest increase shown by any other founder, the remaining seven rising as B3's loss is redistributed. Whatever variation is responsible therefore sits at low frequency in the population and is carried on a single founder haplotype, rather than being spread in a graded way across the eight.

It does not, however, behave as an unconditionally deleterious allele. The ten cages were established in sequence, so their unselected control samples are also a series in time (Fig. 2h). Across that series B3 is flat at 13.2 to 16.2%, with no trend (Spearman rho of frequency on cage order 0.26), while over the same cages B6 falls from 59.2 to 50.4% (rho -0.90) and B5 rises from 2.4 to 5.8% (rho 0.92). The founder that is strongly selected against among the long-lived is not the one losing ground in the cage, and the founders that are drifting in the cage are not the ones that respond to selection on lifespan. A haplotype that reduces an individual's chance of reaching the longest-lived tail by more than half is nonetheless holding its frequency in the population that produced those individuals, which is what would be expected of an allele whose cost is conditional rather than one that is simply unfit.
